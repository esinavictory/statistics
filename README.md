# statistics
## Applied statistics: hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and research analysis.
### Confidence intervals and hypothesis testing
#### Gender pay gap
- Data: RLMS 2019, sphere of education, Moscow (n = 30).
- Methods: confidence intervals of the average salary by gender (90/95/99%); interval for the difference of the average independent samples with different variances; t-test.
- Result: t = 0.51 — there is no significant difference in salaries. The intervals for men are wider due to the small subsample (n = 6).
#### Internet among the elderly (60+)
- Data: own survey, n = 88.
- Methods: power analysis (power.prop.test); hypothesis testing about the proportion of users.
- Result: z = 6.18, h₀ (p = 0.5) is rejected. 83% of the respondents use the Internet, contrary to the stereotype of low digital literacy of the elderly.
#### Family and work
- Data: RLMS 2020, age 25-45.
- Methods: confidence intervals and a Z-test for the difference in the proportion of employed people among married (p₁ = 0.77) and unmarried (p₂ = 0.74); gender breakdown.
- Result: For the entire sample, Z = 1.80 — the difference is significant by only 10%. When broken down by gender, the effect is the opposite (men have higher employment among married people, women have higher employment among unmarried people), so the effects cancel each other out in the aggregate.
#### Before and after the pandemic
- Data: RLMS, waves 2017 and 2022; related samples by ID, people with an initially elevated BMI.
- Methods: testing the hypothesis of an average change in BMI (H₀: μ = 0).
- Result: On average, BMI decreased significantly — the group's health improved according to this indicator; in women, the changes are more pronounced.

### Research analysis and survey design
#### Critical analysis of the publication
Digital media and children’s intelligence (Scientific Reports, 2022, ABCD sample, ~5,000 children). Analysis of the sampling method (stratified random) and sources of bias (response / non‑response bias, pandemic effect). Conclusion: stratified sampling is more justified than quota sampling; accuracy would be improved by taking into account the genre of games and by objectively measuring screen time.

#### Trends based on RLMS data.
Analysis of the RLMS sample design (multistage stratified probabilistic) and study design: formulation of the question, justified division into age groups, consideration of the gender dimension.

#### Own survey using three methods:
Random, convenience, and quota samples; the topic is the desired number of children among Generation Z.
- Chi‑square test for the relationship between the number of children in the parental family and the desired number of children.
- Z-test for equality of desired number of children between men and women: Z = 0.53, p = 0.60 — no differences.
- It is shown how the sample selection method changes the result.
#### Development of a falsified study
A training exercise to recognize manipulations: biased samples, leading questions, manipulation of results.
